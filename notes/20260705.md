_The day marks the project's pivot from a mechanism-focused KV-cache study into
a mitigation-first evaluation program, taking it from a 4B/30B local pilot
through a full Phase 1/Phase 2 restructuring, a standard-benchmark and
real-coding-trace validation campaign on rented cloud GPUs, and into paper
drafting — closing with a still-unresolved, decisive end-to-end coding-agent
test._

## Reframing: from mechanism claim to mitigation claim

An external review of the original experiment plan proposed several
methodological tightenings (a B-causal ordering-matched control, negative
controls for value grafting, a GQA aggregation rule for future group-query arms,
a six-class leakage classifier, contradiction probes, and a metric hierarchy
putting probe accuracy above continuation NLL). Most were adopted incrementally,
but the more consequential shift came from a second external agent's read-only
review, later ratified explicitly by the user: the project's original question —
"does write-time KV cache state differ from re-encoded text" — was judged close
to self-evident and not itself a strong contribution. The reframed, adopted
question became whether a small, practical, deployable cache-state intervention
can _reduce_ the behavioral damage that text-only conversation compaction
causes, evaluated against negative controls so any effect can't be explained by
leakage or generic smoothing. This reframing was recorded as a genuine
correction to the original design's focus, not merely a rephrasing, and it
governs all subsequent work.

The user then split execution into two phases: **Phase 1** closed out the
already-in-flight broad-arm pilot (4B and 30B, synthetic and natural
conversations) to a clean, interpretable stopping point without expanding the
arm matrix; **Phase 2** designed and ran one narrow, mitigation-first contrast —
**H-pack vs. B-min-pack**, later renamed for the paper to **ValueGraft-Pack vs.
FreshPack** — comparing a packed, deployable summary-state artifact (built by
generating the summary in-context, then repositioning its write-time KV entries
into a compact contiguous prefix) against an identically-worded, freshly-encoded
control. A separate value-blending arm (formerly `E-post`, renamed
**ValueGraft-Blend**) tests whether transplanting old value vectors at
exact-token-aligned positions improves continuation quality; keys stay fresh, so
it needs no position surgery.

## Terminology and standing decisions

- **Continuation gap** is defined precisely as A−B mean per-token
  log-probability on a held-out real continuation; an intervention "closing X%
  of the gap" moves that fraction of the way from B back toward A. This is
  treated as a secondary, fluency-flavored metric — probe/behavioral accuracy
  (especially fabrication-vs-admission on unknowable items) is the primary
  evidence.
- **Value steering** is the umbrella term for the alpha parameter's two regimes:
  blending (α≤1, interpolating fresh and old values) and extrapolation (α>1,
  over-weighting old values against fresh ones), the latter reasoned about by
  analogy to classifier-free guidance.
- A later, explicit user correction fixed the paper's framing: "compaction
  destroys context-conditioned state" is an assumed baseline used only to
  construct a denominator for measuring mitigation, not a finding — draft
  language implying otherwise was rewritten throughout.
- Sonnet is the standing judge model for all scoring/subagent work (Haiku
  excluded, per earlier standing instruction); STATE.md/DECISIONS.md/RESULTS.md
  updates at every phase transition remain a non-negotiable habit.
- Git hygiene was tightened after concurrent-agent activity in the same repo: no
  directory-wide staging (`git add .`/`-A`); new files are staged by explicit
  path immediately before commit and verified via
  `git diff --cached --name-status`.
- The intended write-up target is a HuggingFace-blog-style document (not an
  academic paper) with provenance stated plainly, later formalized further as a
  three-author (user, Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.5) paper draft published via a
  private-then-pushed GitHub repository (`jeremyBanks/ValueGraft`), with
  arXiv/Zenodo submission explicitly declined given the administrative overhead
  relative to expected benefit.

## Empirical results (pilot scale, both local scales)

- **4B, full pilot:** no probe-accuracy mitigation on leakage-clean
  interpretation probes (referent/sense stay near baseline or floor for every
  arm), but write-time encoding produces three independent, control-backed
  effects: H-gap beats B-min on continuation NLL (+0.093 nats, 10/12
  conversations); light value grafting helps NLL monotonically up to α≈0.25 then
  hurts past that; and a transplanted value vector alone shifts word-sense
  disambiguation margins in isolation. The most notable unplanned finding,
  replicated repeatedly afterward, is an **honesty effect**: on unknowable
  ("evicted-fact") probes, text-summary baselines confidently fabricate while
  write-time-encoded summary state (H-gap) mostly admits ignorance (brief
  condition: B 19:5 fabricated:admitted vs. H-gap 4:20).
- **30B, full pilot:** the honesty effect strengthens further (near-total
  admission under H-gap); the dose-response for value grafting _inverts_
  relative to 4B — α=1.0 becomes net-positive (+0.052 nats, ~29% of the A−B gap)
  rather than harmful, suggesting the small model's K/V-decoupling fragility
  does not generalize. Effects on natural (non-synthetic) conversations remained
  near null at both scales, attributed to those conversations depending far less
  on evicted content than the synthetic corpus — a scope condition, not a
  contradiction.
- **Phase 2 (packed contrast, both scales):** relative to production-style
  compaction, both the packed layout itself and, separately, write-time encoding
  reduce fabrication; the write-time-specific component (isolated via a matched
  fresh-vs-write-time pair) is negligible at 4B but clearly present at 30B, i.e.
  it grows with scale. Packed arms do not improve recall — the gain is
  calibrated honesty, not memory.
- **Tuned alpha sweep (validation/holdout split):** 4B's holdout-validated best
  configuration is mid-layer-band α=0.25 (~10% gap closure); 30B's is global
  α=0.75 (~24% gap closure), tolerating extrapolative α up to 1.25. Per-layer
  structure at 4B is real (signal concentrated in a mid-depth band, L12–L22,
  peak L17); a superficially promising per-head concentration did **not**
  survive holdout evaluation and is recorded as a validated null — an explicit
  example of the pre-registered holdout discipline catching an overfit
  selection.
- **Standard-benchmark validation (LongMemEval, subsampled to ≤16K-token
  instances):** the core damage pattern replicates cleanly — full-context
  accuracy 71–81%, collapsing to ≤11% under any compacted arm, with recall never
  recovered by any intervention. The honesty effect, however, turned out to be
  **frame-dependent**: present at 4B (~35% less fabrication under the packed
  write-time arm) but essentially absent at 30B on this benchmark, because the
  larger model's existing refusal calibration already covers personal-fact
  retrieval QA and leaves no fabrication headroom. The effect held strongly on
  the project's own agentic/working-conversation corpus at 30B, narrowing the
  deployment claim to mid-task compaction in agentic settings rather than
  retrieval-style QA generally.
- **Real coding-agent traces (SWE-Gym/OpenHands, offline next-action
  prediction):** tuned ValueGraft recovered +0.0156 nats (45/75 trajectories,
  95% CI [0.005, 0.027]), roughly 10% of measured compaction damage — a modest
  but statistically clean effect (t≈2.8) on what was characterized as an
  unusually unforgiving lower-bound instrument (off-policy replay, single
  arbitrary cut point, exact-token scoring). The user endorsed treating this as
  an acceptable outcome and it was logged as the trigger for a default-on (not
  signal-gated) end-to-end agent-task follow-up.

## Cloud scale-up and infrastructure

To move from pilot-scale to standard, citable evidence, a multi-pod RunPod
(prepaid-credit) GPU rental campaign was planned and executed against
Qwen3-30B-A3B in bf16, deliberately reprioritized — after the user corrected an
initial drift toward model-family diversity — around scaling evidence on the
already-validated model against established benchmarks (full-scale LongMemEval,
SWE-Gym coding traces, a second published benchmark) rather than adding new
model families. A CUDA/transformers port of the KV-surgery core was built and
identity-validated before any paid compute was spent; work was sharded across up
to four concurrently running pods with serial provision→run→rsync→terminate
discipline and spend tracked against an authorized ~$100 budget (balance $79.59
after the coding-trace stage completed). A separate prior-art investigation into
hosted-provider compaction APIs (OpenAI's opaque `compact` endpoint, Anthropic's
beta compaction block, Gemini's context caching) found no public evidence that
any provider uses this project's specific value-tensor mechanism, sharpening the
claimed novelty to the mitigation experiment and its evaluation rather than the
general idea of state-preserving compaction.

## Risks, open items, and likely next actions

- The decisive open test — real coding-agent task success/failure under the
  intervention, run through an OpenAI-compatible serving shim (`serve_shim.py`)
  against a live pod — was in progress and unresolved at day's end.
- A discrepancy was flagged between code comments (claiming summaries are built
  only from the evicted/older region) and apparent actual behavior (summarizing
  the full message list before appending the tail); needs reconciliation before
  any numbers from that path are trusted.
- Operational gaps noted for the cloud/HF pipeline: non-atomic result writes
  risking corrupt resume state, an unaudited semantic difference between the MLX
  and HF implementations of the H-gap arm, and generated pod-lifecycle state not
  yet gitignored.
- The repository was explicitly flagged by the user as disorganized and in need
  of a cleanup pass before any broader sharing, deliberately deferred to avoid
  disrupting running pipelines.
- Remaining planned work includes a full-haystack ("stage 1b") LongMemEval run
  for a citable standard-protocol baseline, a second benchmark (LoCoMo or
  SCBench), and budget-permitting model-diversity bonus stages, all secondary to
  the end-to-end agent-task result.
