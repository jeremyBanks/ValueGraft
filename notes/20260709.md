## Notes tooling and archive pipeline

The conversation-note and archive-normalization tooling received a substantial
round of fixes, largely triggered by actually running a full regeneration rather
than just describing planned changes:

- A 6-hour conversation-span duration policy was added (split on the largest
  inter-message gap between 4–5 hours, falling back before 6 hours), which
  retroactively split 10 existing notes into 26–27 during regeneration.
- Compaction-scaffolding messages (Claude Code's app-generated "continued from a
  previous conversation" blocks, structurally marked `isCompactSummary`) were
  identified as being fed into the summarizer as ordinary user text and were
  filtered out, cutting ~4.25% of prompt characters across existing notes.
  Codex's differently-structured compaction rows were already excluded by its
  extractor.
- A persistent archive-naming gap (non-contiguous trailing counters from a prior
  rule change, 101 of 112 files needing renaming) was cleared via a bulk,
  single-commit rename pass backed by a new date cache, after which normalizer
  dry-run time dropped from seconds to ~0.06s.
- A two-layer meta-summary system was built: per-UTC-day summaries at
  `notes/YYYYMMDD.md` (this document's format) and a whole-project
  `notes/README.md` synthesizing all daily summaries into a continuous
  history/state narrative. Both are staleness-gated by manifests keyed on source
  git blob IDs, excluded from ordinary archive-naming, and run `deno fmt` before
  hashing. A shared, git-ignored, dotenv-configurable sensitive-topic filter was
  added across all generator layers.
- Two correctness bugs surfaced during a live regeneration run: a
  participant-block sync regex that could delete note content following a
  non-roster `**Participants:**` paragraph (corrupted a batch of notes; reverted
  and repaired), and a rename-chain bug where two files swapping target paths in
  the same batch failed to plan correctly (fixed via temporary-path routing). A
  related instability — renamed conversation notes oscillating between
  successive normalizer runs — was traced to git rename history being an
  unreliable date source after multiple renames; the fix seeds dates from the
  conversation manifest's canonical first-message date instead.
- A repo-local git identity change (intended to attribute future commits to a
  Codex identity) unexpectedly reattributed all subsequent commits repo-wide; it
  was caught, reverted, and the guidance going forward is to use per-command
  author/committer environment variables or commit trailers instead of
  repo-level git config.
- `notes/AGENTS.md` was rewritten to clarify that agents may freely
  add/edit/move files, but archive naming, prefixing, manifest updates, and
  summary regeneration are owned by the scripts, not manual agent action.
- As of the excerpt's end, a live-tail dry run had found two 2026-07-09
  conversation notes with substantial new content requiring revision, and the
  final revision plus commit/push of the day's summary refresh were still in
  progress — this document does not confirm that push completed.

## Cross-architecture reproduction research

The QK-norm ablation approach (disabling QK-norm modules to test whether it
predicts a graft's sign) broke model generation outright — one model hit the
competence floor, another produced degenerate output — and was judged
unrescuable even via a graded-λ salvage, since model collapse and a genuine sign
flip are indistinguishable that way. Re-asking Fable the underlying question in
an unanchored form (rather than the leading "how do we salvage this ablation")
reversed its prior guidance: H1 (QK-norm predicts sign) is already contradicted
by Mistral's positive no-QK-norm result and should be reported as a
pre-registered null at zero further cost. This leading-question failure mode was
logged as **incident #37**, with a standing rule to periodically re-ask Fable
open, unanchored trajectory questions rather than only decision-specific ones.

Fable's unanchored review also surfaced the more consequential risk to the
paper: the headline referent-dissociation effect (+0.10) is based on only 12
hand-authored conversations, and an earlier attempt to double the corpus
(c13–c54) did not reproduce it (referent ≈ +0.009). This reframed the priority
as reproducing the headline effect on held-out fresh conversations (c13–c36)
using a block design (matched baseline + fresh in one homogeneous run), with a
pre-committed extension rule (extend to c25–c36 only if the initial n=12 cell
spans zero).

While executing this pivot, a resource-sizing failure occurred: after ~6.3 hours
rendering 24 conversations, the run was killed ~10 minutes from completion by a
hardcoded `MODEL_TIMEOUT` formula never validated against actual
per-conversation duration, and because the newer cross-architecture harness
writes results only at the end (unlike earlier phases' incremental
per-conversation saves), the full render was unrecoverable. Logged as **incident
#38**, this was independently identified by Fable as the project's second
resource-sizing incident (after #30). The fix — per-conversation incremental
checkpointing plus permanent full-render text persistence — was still in
progress and gating the reproduction re-run as this excerpt ends; render
persistence was elevated to an absolute standing rule (save and commit every
render) because it also makes future re-analysis (a depth-band "rescue test" for
sign-flipping, cross-model render reuse, further per-layer tuning) a cheap
forward pass rather than a re-render.

Separately, a bootstrap check on the judged (as opposed to logprob) metrics
found the SENSE arm significant (+12pp, CI [+2.2, +22.9]) while the REFERENT arm
was not (+9.7pp, CI [−6.9, +26.2], n=18) — read alongside the logprob metric
(where referent is significant and sense underpowered), this establishes the
referent/sense dissociation as real but metric-dependent, a caveat now required
in any writeup.

A $50 top-up brought total budget to ~$100, with spending strictly ordered: core
reproduction first, then completing the two architecture-pole runs, then a
tiered model-enrichment sweep (easy/high-confidence architectures before hard
ones requiring backbone extraction or newer library versions), then two capped,
near-free extensions once renders are banked — the depth-band rescue test and a
text-only cross-model render-compatibility check (feeding one model's rendered
text to another) that directly probes whether the project's "self-native" scope
claim holds or is more general.

## Framing-history correction

The project owner clarified that the mitigation-first framing (deployable
intervention for compaction damage) was their intent from the start, not a
course-correction from an earlier "mechanism is the finding" framing as prior
generated summaries (`notes/20260705.md`, `notes/README.md`) narrate it. That
earlier framing was the agents' misunderstanding of the owner's original
request, only corrected once discovered — not a project pivot. This is recorded
as an additive correction rather than a rewrite of existing summaries, and
reinforces the standing rule that "compaction destroys context-conditioned
state" is baseline/denominator, never a headline finding.
