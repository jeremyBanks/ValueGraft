## Research and workflow developments

The day opened with continued tau-bench integration work, targeting a
banking-knowledge domain via a retrieval-augmented harness. After real
integration effort (dependency fixes, a working retrieval path, a first
end-to-end episode), pilot runs revealed a structural limitation: conversations
in this domain were too short to create genuine early-content-eviction pressure
at any reasonable compaction threshold, even after lowering the threshold and
swapping in a stronger simulator model. This was recorded as a scope-boundary
finding about the domain rather than a project failure, and the tau-bench line
was retired.

In parallel, the synthetic "chain" agent-task experiment reached closure. A
completed grid across four seeds and five configurations confirmed a
per-layer-tuned ("champion") grafting configuration eliminates an instability
seen under naive full-strength grafting (which had collapsed entirely on one
seed). However, the plain compacted condition also passed uniformly across all
seeds, meaning the chain tasks showed no detectable compaction damage at the
binary pass/fail level — a ceiling problem that left no visible damage for
grafting to repair. A designated recall-probe instrument was later found to be
invalid for this task tier (it referenced constants from an earlier task type),
and an effort-count proxy showed no separation between conditions either. With
both supplementary channels ruled out, the agent-task line was parked, all
compute was shut down, and the decision of whether to pursue harder chains,
adopt a different benchmark, or write up existing results was left open.

The most consequential development was a re-analysis of existing data through a
different lens: rather than scoring for exact fact recall, existing model
outputs were re-judged for **meaning recovery** across three probe categories —
disambiguation of an evicted sense, recovery of a specific prior decision
(referent), and recovery of an evicted preference or disposition (stance). This
produced the project's central finding: at 30 billion parameters, value-grafting
recovers a meaningful fraction of lost sense and referent-type meaning relative
to plain compaction, while showing no effect on stance, where a plain summary
already preserves the relevant disposition. The pattern — effect size tracking
where compaction actually caused damage — was read as a signature of a genuine
mechanism. This finding was corroborated by an independent, judge-free
teacher-forced metric (smaller magnitude, same direction), and partially
bounded: it did not replicate at 4 billion parameters, and a same-architecture
check on a second, newer 27-billion-parameter model supported the sense/stance
split but showed a flat (non-replicating) referent effect.

A parallel investigation used a newly available interpretability tool (a
Jacobian-based residual-stream readout, referred to internally as the J-lens) as
a secondary, corroborating instrument. Early exploratory work — narrative demos,
a broad all-token/all-layer sweep, coding-trace readouts — produced illustrative
examples of context-conditioned semantic drift, but a significant methodological
gap was later discovered: the initial readouts only ever compared "write-time"
versus "freshly re-encoded" states, never actually capturing the grafting
intervention itself, due to a cache-structure incompatibility with the newer
model. This was corrected by building a proper multi-condition probe (full
context, fresh compacted, a zero-strength sanity control, and the actual grafted
condition). Results from the corrected probe were modest: the intervention is
measurably alignment-sensitive (a deliberately misaligned control performs
worse), full-strength blending is worse internally than a partial blend, but the
technique does not recover facts a summary aggressively omitted — only reduces
reinterpretation error around facts a summary already retained. A follow-on
free-generation divergence probe, designed with pre-registered outcome
categories to avoid selective reporting, found no clean internal "fork" toward
the correct concept in any case — a clean, honest null that reinforced the
existing cautious framing rather than undermining it.

A separate mechanism investigation asked whether grafting keys (not just values)
could help. A dedicated per-layer sweep found no rescuing effect from
key-grafting on referent-style recovery at any tested layer, closing that line
for semantic-phrase-style targets specifically; a narrower open question about
short, identifier-like recovery targets (where a second, independently-run
investigation suggested a possible small key-grafting advantage) was flagged as
statistically underpowered and deferred to a future, properly pre-registered run
rather than pursued immediately.

Substantial effort also went into finding a better synthetic task shape for
demonstrating end-to-end behavioral benefit. A cheap local design search (small
model, local hardware) screened several candidate task families and identified
two promising lanes: a "private policy label" task (consistently
value-sensitive) and a "named transformation" task (showing a distinct
key-sensitive subset, mainly around identifier and normalization-style
transforms). These were explicitly framed as cheap prospecting results to guide
future task design, not as findings in themselves.

Finally, a self-contained interpretability side-investigation (kept in its own
isolated workspace, with sensitive terms deliberately excluded from filenames

27-billion-parameter model handles a well-known, politically sensitive
historical topic. Behavioral testing found a language- and framing-dependent
pattern rather than uniform refusal: direct English questions were often
answered factually, English framings using an official euphemism drifted toward
official/stability language, and a direct Chinese-language question was answered

was also visible without the specialized tool, tempering claims of the tool's
necessity. All results here were treated as preliminary
behavioral/interpretability observations, not as claims about any deployed
system's policies or mechanisms.

## Decisions and terminology to persist

- The project's grafting technique should be referred to as a value-vector graft
  applied at a compaction/summarization boundary, with keys and values treated
  as independently tunable channels; a "champion" configuration refers to a
  per-layer-tuned parameterization found to cure a specific instability at
  full-strength settings.
- Probe categories are fixed as **sense** (disambiguation), **referent** (a
  specific decision or fact), and **stance** (a preference or disposition) —
  this taxonomy is now the standard lens for evaluating recovery, replacing an
  earlier, coarser fact-recall framing.
- The interpretability readout technique used throughout should be treated as a
  hypothesis-generating, aggregate-level tool with an uncharacterized
  false-positive rate — suggestive corroboration only, never sufficient on its
  own, and any claim it supports should also have independent behavioral
  confirmation.
- A recurring process rule was reinforced: when an artifact or measurement
  result looks structurally surprising or contradicts an established number,
  stop and validate against the producing code before drawing conclusions,
  rather than patching queries or narratives around the surprise.
- Reports built from this project should lead with the primary behavioral result
  and treat supplementary mechanism probes (interpretability readouts, secondary
  architecture axes) as corroborating, secondary material — not the other way
  around.
- Terminology around internal readout instruments was flagged as previously
  overloaded (multiple distinct techniques informally called "the lens") and
  should be kept disambiguated in future writing.

## Empirical results, with caveats

- **Central finding:** grafting recovers meaningful sense/referent-type meaning
  at 30B relative to plain compaction, with a null (correct, expected) effect on
  stance. Corroborated by an independent judge-free metric. Caveat: does not
  replicate at 4B; a second model at comparable scale supports the sense/stance
  split but not the referent effect.
- **Chain-task grid:** a tuned grafting configuration eliminates a full-strength
  instability, but the underlying task set showed no detectable compaction
  damage to repair (ceiling effect) — this result speaks to tuning/stability,
  not to demonstrated damage recovery.
- **Key-grafting:** no rescuing effect found for semantic-phrase-style referent
  recovery at any tested layer; a narrower identifier-style question remains
  open and statistically unresolved.
- **Interpretability corroboration:** modest, alignment-sensitive internal
  shifts consistent with the behavioral story, but the technique does not
  recover facts a summary omitted outright, and a follow-on free-generation
  probe found no clean internal example of the effect despite a design built
  specifically to surface one.
- **Unresolved discrepancy:** a new internal-effect measurement produced a
  headline number sharply contradicting the already-published report figure on
  identical inputs; the original scoring path still reproduces the published
  number, so the newer script is the leading suspect, but this is not yet
  confirmed and blocks further downstream work.
- **Side interpretability probe:** on a sensitive historical topic, model
  behavior varies by prompt language and framing rather than showing uniform
  refusal, with some internal association present even where the surface answer
  avoids it; a plain raw-logit-lens baseline recovered much of the same signal,
  so the specialized tool's added value here is clarity rather than unique
  access.
