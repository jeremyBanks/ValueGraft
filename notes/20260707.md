_On 2026-07-07, ValueGraft’s main evidence was consolidated into a polished
report: moderate write-time value grafting improves compaction-damaged semantic
interpretation at larger scale, while key grafting and omitted-fact recovery
remain limited. The day also exposed unresolved validation conflicts and showed
that the J-lens is best treated as subtle corroborative tooling rather than
decisive mechanistic proof._

**Participants/contributors:** Jeremy Banks (user/director), Claude Fable 5,
Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Sonnet 5 (labeled subagents), OpenAI GPT-5.5, and Gemini
Pro 3.1, whose synthetic-harness proposal was later adapted. Provenance records
clarify that some commits labeled Fable were authored by Opus after the
documented model handoff.

## Main developments

- The behavioral ValueGraft result was sharpened around semantic continuity
  rather than generic recall. At 30B, sense and referent performance improved
  after value grafting while stance remained effectively unchanged; the
  sense/stance dissociation also appeared on Qwen3.6-27B, whereas referent
  recovery was strong at 30B but not replicated at 27B. The 4B control retained
  the honesty/fabrication effect but not the semantic dissociation.

- The canonical intervention is now V-Graft: retain fresh keys and blend aligned
  write-time values across the compaction boundary. K-only, coupled, and
  independent K/V tests did not improve the tested semantic-phrase tasks over
  V-only; uniform key grafting was generally harmful. A small 0.6B screen found
  possible K-sensitive behavior for identifier-like transforms, but this is
  exploratory and motivates, rather than establishes, a future
  morphology-focused test.

- Full-strength grafting can destabilize behavior. Per-layer tuning restored the
  chain champion’s performance: s1 achieved 4/4 with the tuned champion versus
  0/4 for grafting at α=1.0, while the other chains remained perfect across
  arms. The durable interpretation is that tuning prevents graft-induced
  collapse; these chains do not yet demonstrate recovery from compaction damage.

- The chain and tau tracks both exposed benchmark limitations. The current
  chains are too robust and partly contaminated by an invalid inherited recall
  probe. Tau-banking dialogues were too short to create meaningful compaction
  even after simulator improvements, and forced compaction evicted little useful
  context. No further spending was authorized on either track without a
  redesigned pilot or harder cross-dependent tasks.

- The J-lens work was methodologically corrected. Old-context versus
  fresh-summary readouts diagnose a state gap but do not measure intervention
  benefit. Valid comparisons require full, fresh-compacted, aligned-grafted,
  zero-graft, shifted-control, and alpha-sweep conditions with aligned target
  sequences and provenance checks. The corrected artifacts validated 96 aligned
  summary-token pairs and 16 modified value-cache layers.

- Broad Qwen3.6-27B scans found substantial write-time/fresh readout differences
  across 1,175 summary tokens and 63 layers, with layer 48 providing the
  strongest separation while remaining relatively distinct from next-token
  probabilities. Layer 62 was more continuation-like. Qualitative examples
  across game, planning, software, and household summaries suggest that private
  labels, stale identifiers, roles, and decisions can drift toward generic
  lexical meanings when freshly re-encoded.

- Intervention probes showed small alignment-sensitive shifts toward
  full-context state at moderate alpha, worse behavior for shifted values, and
  degradation at α=1.0. A sparse challenge in which the summary omitted the
  relevant relation produced essentially no recovery. Free-generation probing
  yielded 0 decisive forks toward full context, 17 subtle leans, and 26
  disconfirming cases. The J-lens therefore supports a modest claim about
  low-resolution internal movement, not vivid per-example recovery.

- The next-token control established that layer-48 J-lens readouts are not
  simply next-token probabilities in many cases, though late-layer readouts can
  be continuation-dominated. J-lens outputs remain approximate,
  tokenization-sensitive, single-token-oriented, and hypothesis-generating
  rather than validating.

- The report and repository were substantially cleaned and published.
  `REPORT.md`/`README.md` now foreground value grafting, narrow the claim to
  reduced reinterpretation error around facts retained in summaries, distinguish
  behavioral evidence from interpretability evidence, and document scale,
  benchmark, and omitted-fact limits. Operational notes, provenance corrections,
  artifact validators, and pod-shutdown practices were updated.

## Persistent decisions and terminology

- Use **ValueGraft** for the intervention family; use **V-Graft** for
  values-only grafting and **KV-Graft** only when keys are also modified. Keys
  require positional re-rotation; values do not.

- Treat the full-context arm as the ceiling, fresh compaction as the baseline,
  and grafted compaction as an intervention. Old-versus-fresh J-lens comparisons
  must not be presented as intervention evidence.

- Report meaning recovery separately from verbatim continuation recovery. Prefer
  gap closure, teacher-forced logprob, judged meaning, contradiction,
  calibration, or distance-to-oracle measures over binary pass/fail alone.

- Use span-first reporting for paths, commands, identifiers, and multi-token
  concepts. Raw token rankings are supplementary because punctuation,
  formatting, and subword fragments often dominate.

- Keep J-lens material secondary to behavioral results. It reads residual-stream
  content after cache influence; it does not directly read KV tensors or
  establish task success.

- Validate producer schemas before interpreting artifacts, preserve negative and
  contaminated results, and never infer successful remote work merely from
  process creation or GPU activity.

## Empirical caveats and open risks

- A newer placebo-controlled effect-bound implementation disagreed with the
  saved F1 evaluator: the original data reproduce the published positive
  sense/referent results, while the newer 27B/30B runs were near-null or
  negative. This is a critical apparatus discrepancy, not yet a scientific
  conclusion.

- The mandatory next step is to rerun and audit the original F1 evaluator on
  30B, compare token alignment, prompt construction, target scoring, cache
  provenance, and alpha handling, then decide whether the newer probe is buggy,
  measuring a different quantity, or revealing instability in the original
  result.

- Cross-architecture testing remains paused until that discrepancy is resolved.
  Any future pilot should use a shared summary, pre-registered controls,
  normalized gap closure, positive-direction smoke tests, and explicit handling
  of GQA, sliding-window, hybrid, or latent-cache architectures.

- The strongest supported claim is limited: write-time value state can reduce
  reinterpretation error around information still present in the summary.
  Current evidence does not show reliable recovery of relations deliberately
  omitted from the summary, universal benefit from keys, or end-to-end agent
  improvement.

- Likely follow-up work is the F1 apparatus audit, then a bounded
  architecture-aware pilot or a typed policy/identifier benchmark. Harder
  cross-dependent chains and a properly designed tau pilot remain alternatives,
  not authorized runs.
