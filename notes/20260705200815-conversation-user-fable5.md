_This conversation covers completion of the 75-trajectory SWE-Gym replay
evaluation, validation of tuning methodology across model scales, and the
transition toward standard-benchmark and end-to-end OpenHands testing. It also
records current cloud execution state, safeguards, budget, and handoff
priorities._

**Participants:** User and claude-fable-5.

**Concrete result.** Stage 2 completed on 75 real coding-agent trajectories: the
tuned graft recovered +0.0156 nats/token relative to compacted context, with
45/75 wins and bootstrap CI approximately [0.005, 0.027]. This represents about
10% of measured compaction damage and is statistically reliable but modest. The
result supports the claim that value steering recovers a real slice of context
loss on production-shaped traces; it does not establish that compaction is
solved. Token-level likelihood remains an imperfect proxy, and the replay is
off-policy because Qwen predicts actions generated by another agent. The
end-to-end OpenHands run is therefore the recommended follow-up, especially
because a null caused by artificiality would strengthen—not weaken—the case for
an ecological task-resolution test. Only a consistent null across dependence and
behavioral metrics should argue against it.

**Tuning methodology.** Cloud E-arms originally used global α=0.75 selected on
the local 4-bit 30B stack, but a dedicated bf16 tuning track was added on pod-4.
The preregistered design uses axis-aligned slices rather than an exhaustive
high-dimensional sweep: global α, per-layer profile, and per-KV-head profile,
followed by a small number of low-degree-of-freedom rules evaluated once on the
holdout. Adoption requires a paired bootstrap CI excluding zero or at least 8/10
holdout wins; if the bf16-tuned candidate is indistinguishable from the
incumbent within roughly 0.005 nats, the bf16 version is still preferred for
stack correctness; otherwise α=0.75 is retained. Suspiciously sharp small-slot
winners receive an additional wrong-conversation check.

At 4B, the layer axis showed genuine mid-band structure (approximately layers
12–22), while apparent head structure failed holdout validation: the head-2 rule
fell to 5/10 wins and half the effect, and top-16 slots had inconsistent gains.
The incumbent mid-band α=0.25 rule remained the validated champion. At 30B, the
layer profile was diffuse; the bf16 α sweep showed early evidence that α≥1.0 may
win, motivating an extended bracket through α={1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}. Values
above one are described as extrapolation or contrastive steering, not ordinary
blending. The final 30B per-head profile and holdout verdict were still being
analyzed at transcript end.

**Operational safeguards.** Single failed SSH probes are treated as transient
noise; action requires two consecutive failures or a multi-cycle stall.
Monitoring was consolidated into a staggered, retry-hardened Bash watchdog
(`podwatch.sh`) that reports only genuine stalls, tracebacks, or completions.
Pod model loads now require VRAM-residency verification after a CPU-offload
incident. Local curiosity sweeps are lowest priority and protected by an
auto-kill watchdog at 12% free memory or 28 GB swap; detached pod jobs use
resumable atomic writes. Ollama models and all listed LM Studio models were
deleted with authorization, reclaiming about 59 GiB and leaving roughly 100 GiB
free; a disk watchdog remains armed below 20 GiB.
