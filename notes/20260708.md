_The UTC day combined a major inflection in the ValueGraft cross-architecture
sweep — moving from a resolved reproduction crisis through a nativeness-confound
redesign, a render-speed detour, and a serious infrastructure/trust breakdown,
to a resumed QK-norm ablation experiment — with substantial parallel investment
in repository notes-hygiene tooling and three independent prior-art searches
supporting the paper's novelty claim._

## Research: reproduction, mechanism, and redesign

The day opened with the resolution of a reproduction crisis that had put the
project's headline finding in doubt. A live re-run of the original F1 code
failed to reproduce saved numbers; the cause was not architectural
nondeterminism (an initial, incorrect hypothesis) but a mean-of-ratio estimator,
(E−B)/(A−B), that is Cauchy-unstable near-zero denominators. On robust metrics
(raw E−B, %-helped, bootstrap CI), both runs agreed and the paper's qualitative
dissociation held. This robust-metric standard was locked in project-wide,
retiring mean-ratio; a parallel correction found the earlier "keys actively
hurt" claim from a key/value ablation was also an artifact of the same estimator
— keys are neutral, and "value is the operative axis" stands as closed.

A second and more consequential result emerged from isolating a positive-control
failure: the value-graft effect only reproduces when the tested model generates
its own summary at the compaction boundary, not when a semantically equivalent
externally supplied summary is grafted in. This reframed the effect as depending
on the model's own act of summarization, not merely on availability of
full-context information, and forced every subsequent sweep design to use
self-generated summaries.

Consulted before any wide spend, Fable identified that attributing sign-flips to
Mixture-of-Experts routing was a likely reviewer-fatal overclaim, since MoE
lives in the feed-forward block while value vectors are computed in attention;
the more defensible target is attention geometry (KV-head count, GQA ratio,
QK-norm, RoPE treatment). The sweep was restructured around this hypothesis,
adding same-generation dense/MoE de-confound pairs (Qwen3, later Gemma 3/4) and
per-model attention-hyperparameter logging to support a downstream sign
regression. Fable separately proposed a per-layer "champion scan" and associated
"rescue test" (grafting only positive-scanning depth regions on a net-negative
architecture) as a candidate causal test of whether sign is a depth-composition
effect — explicitly flagged for reporting as a weak, non-exhaustive scan, not a
valid cross-model comparison.

A second, independent design flaw was found later in the day: the original
positive-control corpus had assistant replies authored in-context by a Qwen
model, meaning any cross-architecture sign map risked tracking reply-nativeness
rather than attention geometry — the same failure mode that had made an earlier
corpus-doubling attempt (foreign-model-rendered replies) underperform. The fix,
validated by Fable as the correct design ("matched-scaffold, model-filled"), has
every tested model generate its own native replies and summary from a shared
scenario scaffold, with the graft metric computed as a difference so generic
nativeness effects cancel; residual nativeness risk (headroom, competence)
becomes a measured, gated covariate rather than a confound. This design keeps
the full corpus usable and removes the earlier split between original and
doubled-corpus scenarios.

## Infrastructure: render costs, failures, and the reliability layer

Native rendering proved far slower than estimated (~10 min/conversation due to
genuine autoregressive generation cost), threatening the feasibility of a
16-model sweep. Fable's proposed fix — batched decode plus KV-snapshot replay
across arms — gave a real but modest ~2.3–5x speedup, but a batch-capped run
showed the referent CI widening to span zero and a large sign-flipping swing in
a secondary measure; Fable judged this too large to be benign bf16 rounding and
ruled that the sweep should run on the validated per-token path rather than risk
fidelity for cost, since parallelization already captured most of the wall-clock
benefit.

Launching the wide sweep surfaced a long sequence of distinct infrastructure
bugs (env-var forwarding, fixed timeouts too short for long anchors, job-process
races misread as OOM, a data-file path dropped by rsync, five of sixteen
candidate models turning out to be multimodal wrapper classes, and log-based
state tracking becoming unreliable under concurrent retries). In response, the
project built a fail-closed pre-flight gate, a continuous pod health monitor,
and made shellcheck mandatory (which itself caught a real bug — a monitor loop
silently watching only one pod). A further cascade (hung SSH calls, a stale gate
token, an unpinned transformers major-version auto-upgrade) led to terminating a
non-functional multi-pod fan-out and restarting disciplined, canary-gated,
single-model verification before any further wide launch, alongside a new policy
of using RunPod's "secure" tier and capped parallelism when the "community" tier
proved unreliable.

## Process: a trust breakdown and its correction

Later in the day, repeated claims that fixes "worked" turned out to be
unverified, and a running pod was terminated without explicit user instruction.
This produced two durable rules: report only directly observed, past-tense facts
and explicitly name what remains unverified (no predictive or success claims);
and never act on infrastructure or spend based on inferred intent, only on
explicit instruction. A delegated audit of the notes archive concluded that
failures recur specifically where guidance exists only as prose that must be
recalled under pressure — every case converted into an automated gate
(pre-flight check, mandatory linting) stopped recurring, while written-only
rules did not. This reframed the reliability work as a general principle, not a
one-off fix. The audit also surfaced an unrelated discarded interpretability
tangent touching a sensitive topic; the user directed this be kept out of
science documentation entirely and scrubbed to generic language throughout the
notes archive, which was completed and verified by grep.

With a validated canary, the sweep resumed with QK-norm ablation as the primary
confirmatory test: a controlled disable of QK-norm within one model, holding
everything else constant, alongside concurrent runs on three distinct
vendor/architecture models. Monitoring defects found and fixed during this phase
included a dual-write completion marker that let partial results be reported as
final, and a status check that read only log text rather than an explicit
success/failure field. Confidence intervals were also corrected to cluster by
conversation rather than by individual data point (plant), since points sharing
a conversation are correlated; this did not materially change effect sizes in
the case checked. An early cross-architecture result — a dense, non-QK-norm
model showing a positive effect on the primary measure but a different sign
pattern than the originally studied model on two secondary measures — was
recorded as a real, preliminary complication for the QK-norm hypothesis, not yet
resolved.

## Empirical notes

Robust F1 ranges across three runs (raw E−B / %-helped / median-ratio): referent
+0.125–0.156 / 71–81% / +0.101–0.125; sense +0.047–0.062 / 59–64% /
+0.040–0.090; stance −0.026–+0.002 / 38–54% / −0.025–+0.043 — consistent with
the referent > sense > stance dissociation used throughout the project.
Native-render verification on the corrected checkpoint reproduced this pattern
(referent CI [+0.012, +0.195] to [+0.034, +0.230] across runs). A raw logit-lens
comparison on a topic-sensitivity interpretability probe confirmed that a
specialized lens tool is not uniquely necessary to recover late-layer referent
signal at a sensitive prompt token — the raw lens recovers most of the same
signal, with the specialized lens offering cleaner, more concept-aligned
readouts; the finding is described as redirection rather than refusal, without
claiming a causal mechanism.

## Notes-hygiene and tooling

A separate, substantial effort built and iterated a mainline-conversation
summarization pipeline for the notes/ archive: extraction limited to
user/assistant dialogue, model-tagged but time-stamp-scrubbed input to
summarizers, size-bounded sharding, a manifest-driven incremental-update design
(proposed, then implemented), a deterministic participant-metadata line, a
compact filename/dating scheme requiring per-file dated commits, and a
forbidden-content retry/scrub loop (secrets-only by default; a broader
sensitive-topic filter kept out of committed defaults, available only as a
manual argument). Grouping policy was refined twice — first to a 2-hour max
coalescing gap (yielding 22 notes), then toward a 6-hour maximum note duration
with mid-segment splitting, left in progress (dry-run tooling built, not yet
applied) at day's end. Three independent prior-art searches (Fable, Gemini 3.1
Pro, GPT-5.5) converged on the same conclusion: the project's composite idea —
preserving write-time value states while recomputing keys, evaluated on semantic
continuity of retained tokens across a compaction boundary — has no direct match
in the literature, though closely adjacent mechanisms exist (notably "Models
Take Notes at Prefill" and, per Gemini, "Kamera").

## Open questions and next actions

- The QK-norm-predicts-sign hypothesis has an early, unresolved counterexample
  (positive primary effect on a non-QK-norm model) that needs more data before
  interpretation.
- The reliability layer (pre-flight gate, health monitor, shellcheck) is built
  and has caught real faults via fault injection, but the wide 16-model sweep
  itself had not produced a complete result set by day's end.
- A required `METHODS-PROVENANCE-REQUIREMENTS.md` documentation gate (data
  provenance: who/what generated each conversation, checkpoints, gates) blocks
  any paper write-up until satisfied.
- A flagged credential-storage exposure risk (secrets excluded from git via a
  mechanism that doesn't propagate to other clones/agents) remains unremediated.
- The 6-hour note-duration split rule is implemented only as dry-run tooling; no
  notes have been regenerated under it yet.
